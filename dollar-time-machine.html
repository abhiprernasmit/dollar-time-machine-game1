How to run this in class

This is a historical replay: the market is frozen and you control the clock. Each turn shows students only what was known at that moment. They commit a direction on the dollar, then you reveal what actually happened and why. Because it's history, there's no live-data scramble and it runs identically every year.

Suggested loop (5–7 min per turn):

  • Project the turn. Read the headlines aloud; give 60–90 seconds.
  • Students commit Long / Short / Flat — show of hands, clickers, or the button here — and one-line why tied to a model (UIP, PPP, monetary approach, BOP, risk premium).
  • Click Lock in & reveal. Discuss: who was right, and was their reasoning right?
  • Advance. Do 3–4 turns per session, or the whole arc as a review before the final.

Grade the reasoning, not the P&L. A well-argued call that lost still earns credit; a lucky guess with no model doesn't. The self-scored tally here is just a hook — the learning is in the "why."

Where it lands in your syllabus

Turns 1–10 (2024) drill the asset & monetary approaches — interest-rate differentials, UIP, expectations, the carry trade. Turns 11–12 (2025) set up Ch. 20 crises & the group project: a crowded consensus unwinds, and tariffs move the dollar the "wrong" way — the capstone that the capital account, not the trade account, drives exchange rates.

A note on the numbers

DXY figures are approximate month-end levels compiled from public reporting; a few second-half-2025 points are interpolated between verified anchors (end-2024 ≈ 108.5; Jun 30 2025 = 97.09; Sept 2025 bottom ≈ 96.5; Dec 31 2025 = 97.96). The teaching point is direction and magnitude, not the third decimal. Update any value to your preferred source in the SERIES / TURNS arrays in this file.

Collecting responses into a Google Sheet

Every locked-in call saves a row — time, name, turn, choice, actual move, correct?, and the typed rationale — to a Google Sheet you own. One-time setup (~10 min):

  • Create a Google Sheet (use your @wm.edu account).
  • Extensions → Apps Script. Delete the sample, paste the Code.gs I sent, and Save.
  • Deploy → New deployment → Web app. Set Execute as: Me and Who has access: Anyone, then Deploy and authorize.
  • Copy the Web app URL and paste it into SHEET_ENDPOINT = "…" near the top of this HTML file's script.
  • Commit the file to your GitHub Pages repo. Done — clicks now stream into the sheet; use File → Download → Microsoft Excel anytime.

W&M Workspace caveat: if your @wm.edu account blocks "Anyone" access to web apps, either set access to "Anyone within William & Mary" (students must be signed into their W&M Google account) or host the script from a personal Gmail account.
