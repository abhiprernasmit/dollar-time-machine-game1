What is the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY)?

The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) measures the value of the dollar against a basket of six major currencies — the euro (about 57.6% of the basket), the Japanese yen, the British pound, the Canadian dollar, the Swedish krona, and the Swiss franc. It's a single number that answers "is the dollar broadly getting stronger or weaker?"

When the index rises, the dollar is appreciating against those currencies (a dollar buys more foreign currency); when it falls, the dollar is depreciating. Because the euro dominates the basket, big DXY moves are often really euro moves in disguise. In this game you'll predict, month by month, whether the index went up or down — a Buy is a bet the dollar strengthens, a Sell is a bet it weakens.

How to play

Each turn is one month. Read the headlines that were known at the time, then commit your call:

  • Buy dollar — you expect the index to rise that month.
  • Sell dollar — you expect the index to fall.
  • Stay flat — you expect little change / you're hedging.

Add a one-line reason that names a model (interest parity, the monetary approach, the balance of payments, a risk premium). Then Lock in & reveal to see what actually happened and why. Months where the dollar barely moved (under 0.4%) are shown as "≈ flat" and don't count for or against you.

For the instructor

Releasing the quiz in three parts

The 24 months are split into three 8-turn batches. Near the top of this file's script you'll find three switches:

  • const PART_1 = 1; — Turns 1–8 · Jan–Aug 2024 (the rate-differential era, ending with the August carry-trade crash)
  • const PART_2 = 0; — Turns 9–16 · Sep 2024–Apr 2025 (the Fed pivot, the election, the top, and the Liberation-Day tariff twist)
  • const PART_3 = 0; — Turns 17–24 · May–Dec 2025 (the record dollar slide and its late-year recovery)

Set a switch to 1 to include that batch or 0 to hide it, then commit the file. Release Part 1 first, flip Part 2 to 1 later, then Part 3 — all from the same GitHub page, no second site needed. You can also turn on more than one at once.

How to run it in class

It's a historical replay: the market is frozen and you control the clock. Project a turn, read the headlines, give students ~60–90 seconds to commit Buy / Sell / Flat with a one-line reason, then reveal. Grade the reasoning, not the P&L — a well-argued call that lost still earns credit; a lucky guess with no model doesn't. The self-scored tally is just a hook.

Collecting responses into a Google Sheet

Every locked-in call saves a row — time, name, turn, choice, actual move, correct?, and the typed rationale — to a Google Sheet you own. Paste your Apps Script Web-app URL into SHEET_ENDPOINT near the top of the script (full steps are in the setup guide I sent). The indicator top-right shows Saved ✓ / Save failed ✗; the Export CSV button on the final screen is a backup.

A note on the numbers

DXY figures are approximate month-end levels from public reporting. The 2024 path and the 2025 anchors (peak ≈110 in Jan; −10.8% H1; Sept low ≈96.5; five-month high ≈100 in Nov; Dec 31 2025 = 97.96) are well documented; a few in-between second-half-2025 months are interpolated. The teaching point is direction and magnitude, not the third decimal. Every value lives in the editable SERIES array — scoring updates automatically if you change one.
